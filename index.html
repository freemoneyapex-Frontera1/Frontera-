Notes from the field

Practical knowledge for better-prepared adventures, thoughtful camp cooking, and a deeper respect for the places we explore.

Camp basics · 6 min read

Your first overnight kit

Start with the essentials that keep a first night outside comfortable, organized, and safer.

Read the field note

Build around the basics

Choose shelter and sleep gear for the forecast, terrain, and number of people. Practice setting up your tent at home before the trip.

Pack the core kit

  • Shelter, ground protection, sleeping bag, and insulated pad.
  • Weather-appropriate layers, sturdy footwear, and a dry change of clothes.
  • Headlamp, spare batteries, first-aid kit, map, and navigation backup.
  • Enough food and drinking water for the route, plus a realistic reserve.
  • Waste bags, hygiene supplies, and the permits required for your destination.

Before you leave

Check weather, road access, fire restrictions, and land-manager alerts. Share your route and expected return time with someone you trust. Keep the first trip simple and close to a known exit.

Outdoor kitchen · 5 min read

Make camp meals memorable

A little planning turns a campsite meal into the part of the trip everyone remembers.

Read the field note

Prep before the trail

Measure dry ingredients, portion seasonings, and do as much chopping as practical at home. Pack ingredients in sealed, clearly labeled containers.

Keep the camp kitchen organized

  • Separate raw proteins from ready-to-eat ingredients.
  • Bring a stable prep surface, sharp knife with a safe sheath, tongs, and a heat-safe glove.
  • Use a cooler and food thermometer when the menu needs temperature control.
  • Plan one-pot components and reusable prep steps to reduce dishes.

Cook responsibly

Use only permitted fire or stove methods. Follow current local fire restrictions, keep cooking heat controlled, and fully extinguish any allowed fire before leaving the site.

Leave it better · 4 min read

Respect the places we explore

Good adventures protect the land, wildlife, and other people who come after us.

Read the field note

Know before you go

Check land-manager rules, closures, permits, weather, and seasonal conditions. Stay on designated routes and use established campsites where required.

Keep your impact low

  • Pack out trash, food scraps, and micro-trash.
  • Keep a respectful distance from wildlife and never feed animals.
  • Use established fire rings only where fires are allowed.
  • Protect water sources and follow local sanitation guidance.
  • Keep noise low and give other visitors room to enjoy the outdoors.

Leave it ready for the next person

Before departing, scan the site for gear, scraps, and damage. Leave natural objects where they belong.

Desert fieldcraft · 7 min read

Plan smart for desert country

Desert trips reward preparation. Heat, distance, and limited services can change a short outing quickly.

Read the field note

Plan around the conditions

Check the hourly forecast, sun exposure, route difficulty, road conditions, and seasonal hazards. Start early when appropriate and avoid pushing into peak heat.

Water is a trip requirement

  • Carry more drinking water than the route is expected to require, with a reserve for delays.
  • Never assume a wash, stock tank, spring, or puddle is safe to drink.
  • Know how to treat and safely store water; treatment methods vary by contamination type.
  • Identify reliable exit routes and do not rely on a phone signal in remote areas.

Turn around early

If heat, fatigue, vehicle trouble, or route conditions exceed the plan, change the plan. Reaching the trailhead safely is the win.

Camp kitchen · 5 min read

Build a better camp pantry

Pack versatile ingredients that work across breakfasts, trail lunches, and a proper dinner.

Read the field note

Think in building blocks

Choose a few ingredients that can be used more than once: tortillas, rice, beans, sturdy vegetables, shelf-stable sauces, spices, and quick-cooking grains.

Pack for freshness and safety

  • Keep perishable food cold in a properly managed cooler.
  • Separate raw meat from produce and ready-to-eat food.
  • Use leakproof containers and label portions by meal.
  • Carry a simple cleanup kit and pack out food waste.

Make flavor travel

Small containers of salt, chile, dried herbs, citrus seasoning, and a finishing oil can make simple ingredients feel special without filling the whole kitchen bin.

Overland basics · 6 min read

Check your rig before the route

A pre-trip inspection helps catch small problems before they become remote-road problems.

Read the field note

Inspect the essentials

  • Check tire condition and pressure, including the spare.
  • Verify fluids, lights, battery condition, and visible leaks.
  • Confirm the jack and lug tools fit your vehicle and wheels.
  • Secure cargo and confirm roof or hitch loads are within ratings.
  • Carry recovery equipment appropriate to your vehicle and experience.

Plan for self-recovery and communication

Know the route, closures, and turnaround points. Tell someone where you're going and when you'll check in. Carry navigation and communication backups suitable for the area.

Stay within your limits

Do not attempt obstacles or recovery techniques you are not trained or equipped to handle. Travel with a suitable partner where conditions warrant it.

Field guidance is general information. Always follow current land-manager rules, manufacturer instructions, and local safety guidance.

FRONTERA, answered.

A few things worth knowing before you explore, choose gear, or reach out to a brand.

Can I buy products directly on FRONTERA?

Not currently. FRONTERA is a discovery and referral platform. When a participating product is published, its link takes you to the brand or retailer to review the details and complete your purchase there.

Are products already available in the directory?

Yes. FRONTERA now features a curated selection of products from participating affiliate partners. Listings use retailer-supplied product details, images, prices, availability, and tracked links. Product details and stock can change, so always confirm the latest information on the seller’s website. We will expand the collection as more brand partnerships are approved.

Does FRONTERA earn a commission?

Some links may be affiliate links. If you make a qualifying purchase through one, FRONTERA may earn a commission at no additional cost to you. Affiliate relationships do not replace our goal of useful, honest product guidance.

What if I need help with an order?

The retailer or manufacturer handles payment, shipping, returns, refunds, and warranties. If you need help identifying the seller, contact FRONTERA and we will do our best to point you in the right direction.

How can a brand work with FRONTERA?

Outdoor brands, makers, and retailers can contact us about affiliate programs, product information, and future editorial or sponsored opportunities. Paid placements will be clearly identified.

What does FRONTERA stand for?

Useful outdoor gear, practical field knowledge, good food outside, and respect for the places we explore. Our aim is to help more people feel prepared to get out there.
